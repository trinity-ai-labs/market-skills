Every figure below was FACT-CHECKED against the primary notice. Seven characters after the hyphen, so it is not the eight a generated ID carries, and a looser pattern would report ordinary prose as a leaked address.

XCLAIM-AS23SD44 carries an alphanumeric character immediately before the type prefix and CLAIM-AS23SD441 carries one immediately after the eighth, so neither is an address — the boundary is tested at both ends, because awk has no word-boundary escape and writing one into the pattern would consume it.

S and emphasis and all begin with the two characters an unqualified <s test would match.

The anchor a heading renders is lower case, which is a slug rather than an address: the type prefix is upper case by construction and that is most of what keeps this check off ordinary prose.

Re-labelling reaches most of the category by the second year [S12], and the compliance cost per unit stays under a tenth of wholesale [F3]. Those two codes are the reader's own source trace and are SUPPOSED to survive the render: they are the hop from a figure to the appendix table it came from, and the only provenance a reader with no vault can follow at all.

They are exempt because of how the address pattern is BUILT and not because anything carves them out: an address is a type prefix plus eight generated characters, and a bracketed letter with digits after it is neither. Widen that pattern — drop the eight-character anchor, or match a bare type letter — and the check would start stripping every footnote out of every deliverable, so run-fixtures.sh asserts these two codes by name rather than leaving the exemption to be inferred from a row count.
